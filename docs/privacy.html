Privacy policy

Last updated 28 October 2026

machen is a free, open-source app for the Mac menu bar that shows your Google Calendar events and Google Tasks and lets you change them. It's made by Bernhard Rieder (berno.rieder@gmail.com). This policy explains what data machen accesses, what it does with it, and how you can remove it.

In short: machen runs entirely on your Mac and talks directly to Google. There's no machen server. The developer doesn't receive, collect or store any of your data.

Google user data machen accesses

When you connect a Google account, machen asks for these permissions. You can see them on Google's sign-in screen, and machen asks for nothing else.

PermissionDataWhy
See the list of Google calendars you're subscribed to
calendar.calendarlist.readonly		 Each calendar's name, colour, your access level, whether it's shown in Google Calendar, its default reminders, and its id. Your primary calendar's id is your account's email address. To list your calendars, colour their events, know which ones you can edit, show reminders for events that use the defaults, and name the account in machen.

calendar.events		 Each event's title, time, location, description, colour, reminders, video call link, link to Google Calendar, and whether you've declined it. Not the guest list. To show your events, and to add, change, move or delete events when you do so in machen.
Create, edit, organize, and delete all your tasks
tasks		 Your task lists' names, and each open task's title, notes, due date, position and parent task. To show your open tasks, and to create task lists and add, change, move, complete or delete tasks when you do so in machen.

How machen uses this data

  • To show your calendars, events, task lists and tasks in the app.
  • To send the changes you make in machen to Google.
  • To show the reminders you've set in Google Calendar as notifications on your Mac.

machen doesn't use your data for anything else. It doesn't use it for advertising, doesn't sell it, and doesn't use it to develop, improve or train AI or machine learning models. No person reads your data.

Where your data is stored

Only on your Mac:

  • Sign-in: the sign-in tokens Google issues for each connected account are kept in the macOS Keychain.
  • Offline copy: the calendars, events and tasks machen loaded last are kept in a file in your user folder (~/Library/Application Support/io.github.bernorieder.machen/) that only your user account can read, so machen can show them right away and while you're offline.
  • Notifications: upcoming reminders are scheduled with macOS so they appear on time. machen removes them when it quits.
  • Settings: your choices in machen, such as which calendars to show, are kept in the app's preferences on your Mac.

How your data is protected

  • No server in between: your data goes only between your Mac and Google. It never passes through or is stored on a server of the developer, so there's no central copy that could be lost or breached, and no one but you has access to it.
  • Encrypted connections: machen connects to Google only over encrypted connections (HTTPS).
  • Secure sign-in: you sign in on Google's own page in your browser, so machen never sees your Google password. The sign-in uses Google's flow for desktop apps, protected by PKCE and a random state value.
  • Encrypted tokens: sign-in tokens are kept in the macOS Keychain, which stores them encrypted. Other apps can't read them without your permission.
  • Restricted files: the offline copy is a file that only your macOS user account can read. With FileVault turned on, macOS also encrypts it on disk.
  • Limited access: machen asks Google only for the permissions listed above. It can't share or delete calendars, or change who has access to them.
  • Checked app: machen is signed with the developer's Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple, so macOS checks that it hasn't been tampered with. Its source code is public on GitHub.

Sharing

machen doesn't share or transfer your data to anyone. It sends data only to Google, and only to show and change your own calendars and tasks. machen contains no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or tracking.

How long data is kept, and deleting it

The developer doesn't store any Google user data, so there's nothing to keep or delete on any server. On your Mac, machen keeps data only as long as it needs it to work:

  • Sign-in tokens: until you remove the account in machen, which also revokes them at Google.
  • Offline copy: only what machen loaded last: your calendars and task lists, your open tasks, and your events from today up to four weeks ahead. machen replaces it at launch and every 5 minutes while it runs, so events of past days, and tasks you've completed or deleted, drop out at the next refresh. Removing an account deletes its events and tasks from it, and removing the last account deletes the file.
  • Notifications: reminders scheduled with macOS are deleted when machen quits, when you turn off event notifications, and when you remove the last account.
  • Settings: until you change them. A removed account's settings are deleted with it.

Changes and deletions you make in machen happen in your Google account, where Google's own privacy policy applies.

To delete all of machen's data:

  • Remove an account: in machen, open Settings → Sources and click Remove next to the account. This revokes machen's access to that Google account and deletes its sign-in and its data from your Mac. Removing the last account also deletes the offline copy.
  • Revoke access at Google: at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Uninstall: remove your accounts as above, then move machen to the Trash. To remove its settings too, delete ~/Library/Preferences/io.github.bernorieder.machen.plist.

For questions about deleting your data, write to berno.rieder@gmail.com. The developer has no copy of it, but will help you remove it from your Mac.
